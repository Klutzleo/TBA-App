# Tools for the Bad Ass — Core Rules

**Version 3.0** — A Cinematic Tabletop RPG System
Created By: Jason "Klutzleo" Germino — GameOctane.com

## Introduction

First of all, THANK YOU! Just getting to this point means the world to me. Tools for the Bad Ass (TBA) is designed to get you playing fast and reward creative thinking. This isn't about min-maxing stats or memorizing spell lists. It's about telling stories where your choices matter and thinking outside the box gets rewarded.

Every skill you can imagine—no, seriously, ANY skill—is simplified into three core abilities: Intellect, Physical, and Social. No flipping through character sheets to find the right skill. If you want to do it, roll for it. Don't be afraid to try anything.

**What Makes TBA Different**
- **Dice vs Dice:** No difficulty classes. You roll against opponents, so even weaker rolls can succeed if you roll better than the defense!
- **Simple Stats:** Three numbers (1, 2, 3) define your character. That's it.
- **Bad Ass Power:** Bonuses for creative, cinematic actions.
- **Unified Magic & Techniques:** Same mechanics for spells and special abilities.
- **Emotional Stakes:** Tethers and The Calling add narrative weight to dangerous moments.
- **Bonds & Combos:** Relationships earned through play become mechanical advantages.

## Core Mechanics

### Damage Points (DP)

Damage Points represent your character's ability to keep fighting. When you take damage, you subtract it from your DP. At 0 DP, you're knocked out. Going negative can trigger The Calling.

**Negative DP**
- **0 to -9 DP:** You're unconscious and can't act until healed.
- **-10 DP or worse:** The Calling begins. Roll to survive or narrate your final moment.

### The Three Abilities

Every character has three core stats. You assign the numbers 1, 2, and 3 to these three areas. That's your character's baseline; it never changes.

**Intellect (IP)** — Every skill you would use your brain to complete: thinking, investigating, researching, knowledge checks, magic spells, hacking, and strategy. Have fun with it, think outside the box. Don't be afraid to roll an IP check for ANYTHING.

**Physical (PP)** — Every skill you would use your physical body to complete, such as attacking, jumping, climbing, sneaking, etc. But also how your body reacts to things like extreme cold, hunger, poison, and being wounded. You could also use a Physical roll to show off your strength or try to stay sober in a drinking contest.

**Social (SP)** — Every skill to talk to another character or talk your way out of situations. You can use it as charisma or persuasion. Just flexing muscles isn't usually enough to intimidate; you'll need to add a little Social power to get that point across. Pairing a Social roll with either an IP or PP roll can help in a sticky situation.

### Edge

Edge is your experience bonus. It starts at 0 and increases by +1 every even level, reaching +5 at level 10. Through Ascension (levels 11-15), Edge continues to climb to a maximum of +7 at level 15. Edge applies to every roll — skills, combat, magic, and everything. See [LevelingTable.md](LevelingTable.md) for exact numbers.

### Bad Ass Power (BAP)

Bad Ass Power (BAP) rewards creativity and cinematic moments. When you describe an action that's bold, clever, or narratively awesome, the Story Weaver can grant you BAP as a bonus to your roll.

Could it be sliding under a closing door while firing backward? Using the environment creatively, like swinging from a chandelier? Delivering a one-liner before a critical moment? Creating a distraction so the rest of the party can accomplish their goal? Sacrificing yourself to save the world?

These are just examples of cool events that could shape a whole campaign, battle, or an in-the-moment idea you have. If the Story Weaver approves of your badassery, they'll tell you to add your BAP to your roll.

BAP starts at +1 and increases as you level, reaching +5 at level 10. Through Ascension (levels 11-15), BAP continues to climb to a maximum of +8 at level 15. See [LevelingTable.md](LevelingTable.md) for your current BAP bonus.

## How to Play

### The Core Roll

`1d6 + Relevant Ability (IP/PP/SP) + Edge + BAP (if granted)`

You're always rolling against something: an opponent's roll, the environment, or a Story Weaver's difficulty roll.

### Skill Rolls and Checks

Some actions could fit multiple stats. Intimidation could be Physical (flexing muscles) or Social (threatening words). The Story Weaver and player decide together, and have fun figuring out creative combinations!

When there's resistance (an NPC trying to stop you), it's a contested roll — your roll vs theirs. When there's no direct opposition (climbing a wall), the Story Weaver rolls for the environment/difficulty. Think of it like rolling against the situation itself.

## Character Creation

Creating a character takes minutes. No race restrictions, no class limitations. Be who you want.

**Step 1: Concept** — Who are you? A space cowboy with finger guns? A street mage with a vendetta? A diplomat who talks their way through everything? Pick an archetype or create your own. Have fun with it!

**Step 2: Assign Stats** — Assign 1, 2, and 3 to Intellect, Physical, and Social. Your highest number (3) goes to your primary ability, what your character is best at. That's it. These numbers never change; you just add Edge as you level.

**Step 3: Choose Starting Equipment** — At level 1, you start with a weapon (1d6) and armor (Defense Die 1d6). Choose the type based on your character and setting.

Beyond that, your character may have whatever makes sense for who they are and where the story begins. A street detective may have a notepad and a flashlight. A space marine may have rations and a comms unit. A fantasy knight may have a rope and a bedroll. Work with your Story Weaver; if it makes sense for your character to have it, you have it. These items don't grant mechanical bonuses; they're tools for your story.

**Step 4: Play** — That's it. Start playing. Seriously, you're done. Go be bad ass.

## Leveling

Characters level up when the Story Weaver determines they've earned it, usually after major story beats, completed quests, or surviving significant challenges.

**What You Gain at Each Level**
- More DP (increases every level)
- Better weapon/defense dice (check [LevelingTable.md](LevelingTable.md))
- Edge bonus (every even level: +1 Edge, continuing through Ascension)
- BAP bonus (increases with level, continuing through Ascension)
- New magic/technique slot (at levels 1, 3, 5, 7, 9)
- More uses per encounter (3 per level)

Consult [LevelingTable.md](LevelingTable.md) for exact numbers at each level.

### Ascension Levels (11-15)

Level 10 is not the end of a character. It is the end of their apprenticeship. Everything from level 1 to 10 is the hero's journey — the growth, the struggle, the becoming. Ascension is what happens when the journey is complete, and the legend begins.

Ascension levels do not add new abilities. They do not reset the dice progression. They deepen what already exists.

| Level | DP | Edge | BAP | Ascension Unlock |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 11 | 60 | +5 | +6 | Ability 1 — secondary effect |
| 12 | 65 | +5 | +6 | Ability 2 — secondary effect |
| 13 | 70 | +6 | +7 | Ability 3 — secondary effect |
| 14 | 75 | +6 | +7 | Ability 4 — secondary effect |
| 15 | 80 | +7 | +8 | Ability 5 — secondary effect |

**Secondary Effects**

At each Ascension level, one of the character's five abilities gains a secondary effect. The core mechanic of that ability does not change — same dice, same roll, same primary damage or healing. What changes is what the ability now also does.

Secondary effects are defined by the player and Story Weaver together. They are not picked from a list. They must fit within the existing system: attack, heal, buff, or debuff. They emerge from who the character has become.

Secondary effects do not cost an additional use. One use, one activation — just the fully realized version of the ability.

> **Story Weaver Note:** Keep secondary effects narratively consistent with the ability's origin. A technique built on precision should not suddenly cause fire. The effect should feel inevitable. Secondary effects cannot deal damage that, by itself, could trigger The Calling. They enhance. They do not replace.

*Example: Dragon's Ember at level 10 is an IP-based attack. At Ascension 1 (level 11), Scalelord and the Story Weaver decide: every hit now also applies a Fear debuff, because after ten levels of wielding Fyrax's fire, the flame carries the dragon's will. The roll doesn't change. The damage doesn't change. But the target now knows what it is to be seen by a god.*

**Ascension Combos**

When both characters in a Bond have reached Ascension, their named Combo evolves. This is a table event, not a form to fill out. The Story Weaver recognizes it the same way the original Bond was recognized: in a moment, during play.

When the second Bond partner hits Ascension, the SW says, "You two have been through everything. What does it look like now?" The players revisit the Combo together. Does the name still fit? What do the secondary effects add? The name is the players' call. The secondary effects are bound to whatever each character has unlocked through Ascension.

**Level 15: The True Ceiling**

A level 15 character has all five abilities fully realized with secondary effects. Their named Combos are Ascension-evolved. Their DP, Edge, and BAP reflect fifteen levels of growth. They are not superhuman — they are legendary.

Level 15 is a full stop. There is no level 16. The character is complete. What happens next is pure story, not progression.

## Magic & Techniques

Magic and Techniques work identically. The only difference: Magic users rely on Intellect; Technique users rely on Physical or Social.

**Both systems**
- Use the same dice progression (1d6, 1d8, 1d10, 1d12, 2d6, 2d8)
- Limited uses per encounter (3 per level, so 30 at level 10)
- Gained at levels 1, 3, 5, 7, 9

**Using Magic or Techniques**

Magic and techniques roll the same way as attacks. Roll your spell/technique die + Intellect or Physical + Edge.

For attacks, roll against the target's Defense Die. For healing, the roll is the total amount healed. For buffs/debuffs, consult the table below.

**The Five Slots**
- **1st Slot (Single Target):** Your first magic spell or technique, which will become your most powerful.
- **2nd Slot (Area of Effect):** Affects multiple targets. Use carefully, allies can be hit too! It can also be used for mass buffs/debuffs.
- **3rd Slot (Single Target):** Same options as 1st slot.
- **4th Slot (Area of Effect):** Same options as 2nd slot.
- **5th Slot (Single Target):** Same options as the 1st and 3rd slots.

**Healing**

Roll your spell die + Intellect + Edge. The total is how much DP you restore. No contested roll needed.

**Buffs and Debuffs**

Buffs just happen, no rolling needed. You just need to refer to the table below regarding what-sized die for that level spell or tier of item.

Debuffs need to be rolled for and defended against the corresponding stat related to that spell. For example, a Fear debuff that's mind-related: you roll with Intellect, they defend with Intellect. If the Fear is Physical, you roll with Physical.

What buffs or debuffs to use is up to you and your Story Weaver. You can add a modifier to Defense Die or Weapon Die, speed up or slow down a character, blind, confuse, stun, and more.

Buffs and debuffs last the number of rounds equal to the +/- modifier. A +/-2 lasts for two rounds.

**Buff/Debuff Table**

| Die | Modifier |
|---|---|
| 1d6 | +/- 1 |
| 1d8 | +/- 2 |
| 1d10 | +/- 3 |
| 1d12 | +/- 4 |
| 2d6 | +/- 5 |
| 2d8 | +/- 6 |

## Pets, Allies & Summons

**Pets**

Pets are the most basic form of companionship. They can be bought, raised, or found in the wild. They can be anything you want as long as they are within a reasonable size — a dog, a cat, a housefly, a small robot, whatever you wish.

Pets are fun story elements, but they do have a setback: they are unable to assist in battle, and they can die. Think of pets as cosmetic items rather than creatures that grant bonuses.

**Allies**

Allies are much different than pets. These creatures or people have a special bond to you and will go to the ends of whatever planet you're on for you. There is one prerequisite: you'll need to take your first technique for your character as "Ally." This takes your most powerful technique slot, but in return, you gain a very powerful companion.

An Ally could be any creature your mind can think of that fits your game setting. You will be responsible for all role-playing and die rolls for this Ally. Allies level up with your character, though they have their own leveling table. They follow the same death and damage rules as player characters.

**Building Your Ally**
- **Stats:** Assign IP/PP/SP using the same 1/2/3 distribution as player characters.
- **Combat:** Allies use their own stats, Edge, BAP, and dice from the Ally Leveling Table for all rolls.
- **Abilities:** Allies can use weapons, armor, and magical items if it makes sense for their form.
- **Commands:** Allies understand any order you give them, though success may depend on skill checks. They act on their own initiative in combat.

Allies have lower DP than player characters (roughly 80% at each level) to balance their additional combat presence.

**Ally Combos:** A character and their Ally start with one Combo automatically, no earning required. The Bond already exists. If the Ally dies, the Combo dies with them. See *Bonds & Combo Attacks* in the Combat section.

**Ally Death**

If your Ally dies permanently (after The Calling), it causes a negative Tether called Emptiness. This represents the profound loss and grief of losing your bonded companion.

Allies follow The Calling rules just like player characters. They can survive near-death, become Marked by Death, and gain The Called status. If they die heroically, a Memory Echo may be created to honor their sacrifice.

**Summons**

Summons are temporary creatures called through magic and require your first spell slot just like an Ally. They take a full round to summon, and -1 DP per round to maintain. They also match your limitations on how many spells they can use per encounter.

Summons use your spell dice + Intellect + Edge for attacks/healing.

| Character Level | Summon Durability |
|---|---|
| Levels 1-3 | Disappears after 1 hit |
| Levels 4-6 | Disappears after 2 hits |
| Levels 7-9 | Disappears after 3 hits |
| Level 10+ | Disappears after 4 hits |

## Combat

### Initiative

Everyone rolls 1d6 + Physical + Edge. The highest goes first. In the case of a tie, go in order of who has the higher base Physical stat. Still have a tie? Continue to higher Intellect, then Social. If there's still a tie, put whoever brings the best snacks to the play session first. (It's in the rules now. Use this to your advantage, Story Weavers!)

### Making an Attack

Attacks use Weapon Dice instead of 1d6. Weapon dice range from 1d4 to 1d12, depending on your level. See [LevelingTable.md](LevelingTable.md) for what's available at each level.

`Weapon Die + Physical + Edge` vs `Defense Die + Physical + Edge`

If your attack roll beats the defender's defense roll, subtract the difference from the defender's DP. This means even weaker rolls can succeed if you roll better than the defense!

*Example: You have a sword (1d6). You roll 4, add Physical 3 and Edge 1 = 8 total. The enemy has Defense Die 1d6, rolls 2, adds Physical 1 and Edge 1 = 4 total. 8 - 4 = 4 damage to enemy DP.*

**Magic Weapons & Bonuses**

Magic weapons and armor add flat bonuses to your rolls. A +2 sword adds +2 to your attack roll. A +1 shield adds +1 to defense. These bonuses stack with all other bonuses on your rolls.

### Bonds & Combo Attacks

TBA rewards bold choices. Combo Attacks extend that philosophy into something deeper — the relationship between characters. They are not picked from a menu. They are not unlocked by leveling up. They are earned through story, and they carry the weight of everything that came before them.

**What is a Bond?**

A Bond is the Story Weaver's recognition that two characters have built something real together through play. Not declared at character creation. Not earned by reaching a level threshold. Earned by living through the campaign together.

Examples of moments that might earn a Bond:
- One character pulls another back from The Calling
- Two characters survive a fight they had no business surviving
- A shared secret, a vow made under pressure, a sacrifice neither will forget
- A moment so specific to their story that only they would understand it

> **Story Weaver Note:** You decide when a Bond forms. You do not announce it mechanically — you recognize it narratively. When the moment happens, tell them: "You two have something now. Name your move."

Bonds are unlimited. A character can have as many Bonds as the story earns them. Scarcity comes from the campaign, not a rule.

**Naming the Combo**

When a Bond forms, the two players name their Combo together. Not the Story Weaver — the players. It is theirs. It lives in the campaign lore.

The name should mean something. It should come from the story. A Combo named after the moment it was born hits differently than one picked from a list.

*Example: Scalelord and Beef Strongarm survive an ambush by the Ashen Chain Reavers. Scalelord's Dragon's Ember keeps one pinned while Beef finishes the other. The Story Weaver says, "You two have something now. What do you call it?" They name it The Ember Slam. Now it exists. It always will.*

**How Combo Attacks Work**

A Combo is not a single roll. It is two simultaneous ability activations — pre-agreed, designed together when the Bond formed, and executed as one moment.

- The proposer holds their action; their initiative slot skips until the acceptor's next turn comes around
- The combo fires on the acceptor's next turn, and both roll simultaneously at that moment
- Full BAP granted to both automatically, no discretion required
- Effects stack, both damage values apply, both ability effects trigger

The pair designs the outcome at Bond formation. Damage plus damage. Damage plus buff. Heal plus debuff. Two buffs. Whatever fits the story and the characters.

> **Story Weaver Note:** Executing a named Combo is always a Bad Ass moment. Full BAP is not discretionary — it is automatic.

**How Holding Works**

Think of initiative as an infinite repeating list. The proposer declares the Combo on their turn and holds; their slot skips every time it comes around until the combo fires. Everything else in the order keeps moving normally. When the acceptor's next turn arrives, the combo fires and both roll simultaneously.

*Example: Order is NPC → Player 1 → NPC → NPC → Player 2. Player 1 goes normally. When Player 2's turn comes, they propose a Combo with Player 1 and hold. Initiative continues: NPC goes, then Player 1's turn comes back around — that's when the combo fires. Player 2's slot skipped. Player 1's next turn was the trigger.*

**Cancellation**

If the proposer takes damage or drops to 0 DP before the combo fires, the combo is cancelled, and their held action is lost. The acceptor takes their turn normally.

**Combo Use Cost**

Combos use the existing ability use system, no separate cooldown needed.

| Type | Cost Per Character | Total Cost |
|---|---|---|
| Solo ability | 1 use | 1 use |
| Combo (2 characters) | 2 uses | 4 uses total |
| Triple Combo (3 characters) | 3 uses | 9 uses total |

Do you burn the Combo now or save uses for the final boss? That tension is the point.

**Combo Examples**

- **"Bring the Rain"** — Both characters attack the same target simultaneously. Straightforward and devastating. Two damage rolls, both land at once.
- **"The Setup"** — One character debuffs the target's SP, the other fires an SP-based ability in the same beat. The debuff is live when the check hits. The target never had a chance.
- **"Pick Me Up"** — One character attacks to create space, the other heals an ally in the same beat. Pressure and recovery in one move.
- **"The Wall"** — One character attacks, the other buffs the attacker's armor DP bonus simultaneously. Offensive pressure with damage absorption built in.
- **"The Opening"** — One character makes a social or intellect roll to distract or destabilize the target, the other uses the opening to attack. Combos aren't just combat plus combat.

**Ally Combos**

A character and their Ally start with one Combo automatically. No earning required. No Bond moment needed. The Bond already exists; it was forged the moment they chose each other.

If the Ally dies, the Combo dies with them. The Emptiness Tether now carries extra weight — not just grief for a companion, but the permanent loss of a move that only existed between two specific beings. It is gone. It cannot be rebuilt with a new Ally.

**Enemy Combos**

Villain duos and boss pairs can have Combos too. The Story Weaver builds them the same way — a named move between two NPCs who have fought together long enough to coordinate. The Story Weaver does not announce the Combo. They just hold the lieutenant's action. The party figures out why.

> **Story Weaver Note:** The first time an enemy Combo lands on the party it should be a horror moment. The second time they see that lieutenant hold, they will panic. Use this. Let them dread it. Let them plan around it. That is the Combo doing its narrative job.

**Triple Combos**

Three characters. Three Bonds. One move.

A Triple Combo requires all three characters to have reached Ascension (level 10) and to hold bilateral Bonds with each other — three pairs, three existing Combos already established. The three players design the Triple Combo together. It gets a name. It belongs to them.

- The proposer declares the Triple Combo on their turn and holds
- Both acceptors also hold on their respective turns
- The combo fires on the last acceptor's next turn, all three roll simultaneously at that moment
- Full BAP granted to all three automatically
- All effects stack
- Cost: 3 uses per character (9 total across the party)
- If any of the three drops to 0 DP before the combo fires, the combo is cancelled, all held actions are lost

> **Story Weaver Note:** Enemies can have Triple Combos too. A three-NPC coordinated strike built on years of shared history is one of the most powerful tools in your arsenal. Telegraph it. Let the party see it coming. Then let it land.

## Items & Equipment

**Weapons & Armor**

Weapons and armor are defined by their dice. See [LevelingTable.md](LevelingTable.md) for which dice are available at your level; the type of weapon does not determine the roll or the outcome. It is more for flavor until you can get upgraded weapons or armor.

**Multi-Die Weapons**

As you level up, [LevelingTable.md](LevelingTable.md) shows options for multi-die weapons (like 2d4, 3d6, 2d8). These represent burst fire, dual-wielding, heavy weapons, magical enhancement, or any concept that makes sense for multiple strikes.

*How They Work:* Multi-die weapons attack multiple times in a single action. Roll all your weapon dice, add your stats and Edge to each die, and compare each attack to the defender's rolls. The defender must roll defense separately for each attack. Each successful attack deals damage equal to the difference.

*Risk vs Reward:*
- More attacks = more chances to hit (great against tough enemies)
- Rolling low on multiple dice can waste your turn (several weak attacks might all miss)
- Single high-die weapons (1d12) are more consistent — one powerful strike
- Multi-die weapons (3d6) are more volatile — could devastate or disappoint

*Weapon Flavor Examples:* The dice determine mechanics; the weapon description is entirely yours. A 3d6 weapon could be a machine gun, triple-strike sword technique, or conjured magic missiles. A 1d12 could be a greataxe, sniper rifle, or channeled lightning bolt. Choose what fits your character and setting.

At each level, choose ONE weapon die option from your Leveling Table. You can switch weapons between sessions with your Story Weaver's approval.

**Upgraded Weapons & Armor**

Upgraded weapons and armor add flat bonuses (+1, +2, +3) to attack and defense rolls. The Story Weaver determines availability and power, whether through purchase, epic loot, or some other way to earn it.

**Consumables & Tools**

Healing potions, grenades, tech gadgets — these are narrative tools. Work with your Story Weaver to determine effects. Most grant one-time bonuses or heal fixed amounts of DP.

| Tier | Die / Modifier | Healing Potions (max value) |
|---|---|---|
| Tier 1 | 1d6 or +/-1 | 6 DP |
| Tier 2 | 1d8 or +/-2 | 8 DP |
| Tier 3 | 1d10 or +/-3 | 10 DP |
| Tier 4 | 1d12 or +/-4 | 12 DP |
| Tier 5 | 2d6 or +/-5 | 12 DP |
| Tier 6 | 2d8 or +/-6 | 16 DP |

## Environmental Damage

The world is not neutral. Fire burns. The deep ocean crushes. Psychic pressure breaks. Environmental damage gives the Story Weaver a clean, scalable system for making the world itself a threat, not just the things living in it.

**How It Works**

The Story Weaver sets the severity tier of an environmental hazard. The tier determines the damage die. Players may resist with a stat roll; the Story Weaver determines the relevant stat based on the hazard type.

- **PP (Physical)** — heat, cold, physical pressure, poison gas, exhaustion
- **IP (Intellect)** — psychic hazards, disorienting environments, mental pressure
- **SP (Social)** — crowd panic, social pressure, morale-based hazards

| Tier | Damage Die | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Tier 1 | 1d6 | Mild heat, shallow water, light smoke |
| Tier 2 | 1d8 | Open flame, strong current, toxic fumes |
| Tier 3 | 1d10 | Inferno, crushing pressure, severe poison |
| Tier 4 | 2d6 | Volcanic environment, deep sea, lethal gas |
| Tier 5 | 2d8 | World-ending conditions, divine wrath |

> **Story Weaver Note:** You set the tier. You set the relevant stat for resistance. The roll is the damage. Keep it simple; the tier system is designed to be called on the fly without slowing down the session.

## Tethers

Tethers are emotional anchors: beliefs, fears, bonds, or traumas that define who your character is. They're not chosen at character creation. They emerge through play.

A character can have 1-3 Tethers. They evolve as the story progresses.

**Examples of Tethers**
- "I never leave a teammate behind..."
- "Magic destroyed my family..."
- "I will prove I'm more than my father's shadow..."
- "I fear the dark because of what happened there..."

**How Tethers Work**

When a Tether is relevant, it triggers a bonus or penalty. The Story Weaver determines when a Tether activates and chooses a buff/debuff level (1-5) based on how emotionally significant the moment is. Use the same buff/debuff table as Magic & Techniques.

| Level | Modifier |
|---|---|
| 1st Level | +1 / -1 |
| 2nd Level | +2 / -2 |
| 3rd Level | +3 / -3 |
| 4th Level | +4 / -4 |
| 5th Level | +5 / -5 |

*Example: A character with the Tether "Sworn to protect my sister" gets +3 when defending her (3rd level buff), but -2 when separated from her (2nd level debuff).*

Tethers can change. Trauma, triumph, or revelation can create new Tethers or transform existing ones.

**The Grief Tether**

When a Bond is broken — by a partner's death or departure from the campaign — the character gains a Grief Tether (−1) until the Story Weaver lifts it through narrative resolution. The SW sets the weight based on how significant the Bond was. It does not expire on a timer like a combat debuff.

> **Story Weaver Note:** The Grief Tether is a narrative tool, not a punishment. Reward players for processing grief in-character — a ritual, a resolution, a moment of acceptance. When the story earns it, lift the Tether. That moment should feel like something.

This makes leaving a campaign have actual weight for the people left behind. The absence is mechanical, not just emotional.

## The Calling

When a character reaches -10 DP or worse, they enter The Calling, a liminal space between life and death.

This is the moment where your story could end. But it doesn't have to.

**Story Weaver Prompts for The Calling** — ask the player:
- "What do you see in this moment between life and death?"
- "What do you fight to return to?"
- "What unfinished business keeps you here?"
- "If this is the end, what are your final words?"

BAP can be awarded within The Calling for incredible answers.

**The Roll**

`1d6 + Intellect or Social (player's choice) + Edge` vs Story Weaver's difficulty roll

| Character Level | Story Weaver Rolls |
|---|---|
| Levels 1-3 | 1d6 |
| Levels 4-6 | 1d8 |
| Levels 7-9 | 1d10 |
| Level 10 | 1d12 |
| Levels 11-15 (Ascension) | 1d12 — but this is a legend. Play it that way. |

**Outcomes**
- **Win by 1-3:** You survive but are Marked by Death. Take a permanent scar, trauma, or limitation.
- **Win by 4+:** You survive and return to 1 DP. No permanent consequences.
- **Lose:** Your character dies. Narrate your final moment and create a Memory Echo.

**Marked by Death**

If you win The Calling by 1-3, you gain a permanent mark:
- A physical scar or injury
- A trauma or fear
- A limitation (can't use one technique, loses 1 max DP, etc.)

This becomes a new Tether.

**Memory Echo**

When a character dies, their final act leaves a Memory Echo — a lingering effect that alters the story world:
- A dying wish that must be fulfilled
- A secret revealed in their last breath
- A curse or blessing that affects the party
- An item or power is transferred to another character

**The Called**

When you survive The Calling and are Marked by Death, you gain the status "The Called." This represents the lingering connection to death, sometimes manifesting as nightmares, sometimes as prophetic visions.

| Times Called | When It Triggers | Roll |
|---|---|---|
| 1st | Randomly during rests (SW discretion) | 1d6: 1-2 Nightmare (-1 next roll), 3-5 Peaceful sleep, 6 Prophetic vision (+1 next roll) |
| 2nd | More frequent: rests and adventuring | Same 1d6 table |
| 3rd-4th | Very frequent: rests, adventuring, even battle | Same 1d6 table |
| 5th | There is no 5th time. You're permanently dead. | — |

**Clearing The Called Status**

You can keep this status (some players appreciate the chance for the +1), OR you can seek cleansing:
- Complete a spiritual quest
- Visit a holy site or healer
- Perform a ritual (Story Weaver determines requirements)

Once cleansed, "The Called" status is removed, but your death counter remains the same.

## Vehicles

Not every campaign will utilize a vehicle, but if the need arises, you're covered. Vehicles use the Vehicle Leveling Table and can level up alongside the party, representing upgrades, modifications, and the crew's growing mastery.

**Vehicle Leveling**

Vehicles level up when the party levels up. Story Weavers may also grant vehicle levels as rewards for major upgrades or story milestones.

A Level 1 bike doesn't become a battleship; it becomes a badass Level 10 bike with nitro boosters, reinforced plating, and mounted weapons. Vehicles have higher Edge progression (0-10 instead of 0-5) because they represent technological or magical superiority that scales beyond individual skill.

**Vehicle Systems**

Vehicles may have special systems that function like spells or techniques. Vehicle systems use the same spell/technique rules as characters. Crew members activate systems on their turn using their stats + the vehicle's spell die.

**Single-Pilot Vehicles**
- Attack: `Vehicle Weapon Die + PP + Vehicle's Edge` vs Enemy Defense
- Defense: `Vehicle Defense Die + PP + Vehicle's Edge` vs Incoming Attacks

**Multi-Crew Vehicles**

| Station | Roll | Effect |
|---|---|---|
| Gunner (Attack) | Ship's Weapon Die + PP + Vehicle's Edge | Deal damage on a hit |
| Defense Officer | Ship's Defense Die + PP + Vehicle's Edge | Defend against incoming fire |
| Captain (Support) | 1d6 + SP + Vehicle's Edge | Buff Attack or Defense, add result to Gunner or Defense Officer's next roll |
| Pilot (Support) | 1d6 + IP + Vehicle's Edge | Buff Attack or Defense through positioning |
| Engineer (Repair) | 1d6 + IP + Vehicle's Edge | Restore DP to the ship equal to the roll result |

These are suggested stat pairings. If you can justify why a different stat makes sense, discuss it with your Story Weaver. Make it work for your story.

**Vehicle Damage**

Damage goes to the vehicle's DP, not the crew. At 0 DP, the vehicle is disabled. At -10 DP or worse, the vehicle is critically damaged, and catastrophic failure is imminent. The Story Weaver determines what happens based on the situation.
